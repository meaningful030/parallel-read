A slower way
to read the world.

You do not have to understand every word to discover something new. Sometimes, all you need is a little space between the lines.

PParallel Read Journal·3 min read

Reading in another language is a small act of curiosity. It lets us step outside familiar ideas and see the world through someone else's words.

But an unfamiliar sentence can interrupt that journey. We open a new tab, search for a translation, and lose the thought that brought us there in the first place.

Keep the original close.

A translation can be a bridge. When the original stays just above it, we can move between the two languages at our own pace. We understand the meaning without losing the writer's voice.

Good reading is not a race to the last line. It is a conversation with an idea.

This is why a simple bilingual page can make such a difference. Read the English first. Glance at the Chinese when you need it. Return to the original and notice a word you might have missed.

Make room for a little curiosity.

You can start with something small: a short article, a thoughtful essay, or a story about a place you have never visited.

  • Choose a topic you already enjoy.
  • Read one paragraph at a time.
  • Let the translation help you keep going.

Over time, the unfamiliar becomes familiar. Reading turns into a habit, and the world gets a little bigger with every page.
